Hi, I'm Luca. I'm building Colpero, so robots and other machines are easy to deploy and can share what they learn about the places they work in.

Before that I started Augea, which gives AI training data a signed record of where it came from. It began as a hackathon project.

Next is testing Colpero on a real drone, flying over a real site.

Away from work I train jiu-jitsu and go to the gym. If you're curious, my favorite films are on Letterboxd and my favorite books are on Goodreads. Both are missing a lot, I got lazy about filling them in.

How I got here

  1. 2018

    I grew up in a small city in Brazil. I loved football and Barcelona, and I played a lot of Fortnite. At 12 I started reading about economics and politics, because a lot of what I saw around me made no sense to me. That's how I found Bitcoin. I annoyed my family about it until they bought some.

  2. 2018–24

    For the next few years I kept pulling on the same thread, first money and then how technology works underneath. What hooked me was seeing how whole systems fit together, and that's still how I work. Today I design the system and build it with AI.

  3. 2024–25

    I finished school in 2024 and started doing hackathons, mostly because they looked fun. In September 2025 one of them turned into ExchAInge, a marketplace for AI training data that keeps a record of where each dataset came from. We won a few prizes, and I put the money into what I work with today, starting with a good computer.

    Talking to people working on the same problem, I met Ricardo Tavarez (co-founder of NodeKit, backed by a16z crypto) in September 2025. He joined me, and ExchAInge became Augea. In December 2025 I went to Breakpoint in Abu Dhabi, and right after it, following another hackathon win, I became a member of Superteam Brasil, the Solana community in Brazil. I stayed in the region for eight weeks, between Abu Dhabi and Dubai.

  4. Early 2026

    Early in 2026 I spent two weeks in the US: one day of meetings in New York, and the rest at MtnDAO in Salt Lake City. Right after I landed, my Brazilian bank blocked my account, and I figured it out from there. One of the people I met, a robotics researcher in New York, taught me a lot of what I needed to start Colpero.

    Since April I've had 30 calls with people working on robots and drones, most of them researchers and engineers.

  5. Jun 2026

    In June I went to Google with Augea, to get into their startup program.

  6. 2026

    Those calls kept pointing at the same problem. Setting up a few robots in a new place can take a week, and each new machine starts from scratch instead of adding to what the others already know. Soon robots from many different makers will work in the same places, and right now none of them shares what it learns. Colpero is for that gap.

    The same conversations brought me my cofounders. Elijah Young studies mechanical engineering at MIT and does research at the MIT Media Lab. Dhruvil Parikh has a master's in robotics from Northeastern and worked on drone software for NASA programs. Our first prototype took two weeks, and in September the three of us started working on Colpero together.
